Quick Questions

How do I pay?

After you check out, we'll email you to confirm everything's correct and send a payment request (PayPal or Venmo). Nothing is charged automatically — you approve the details first.

How long until my order ships?

Every vane is printed to order rather than pulled from a shelf, so most orders ship within 5–7 business days of payment. If you need it faster for an upcoming hunt or shoot, mention it in the notes field and we'll do what we can.

What if my vane arrives damaged or doesn't fit right?

Email us within 14 days of delivery and we'll reprint or adjust it at no charge. Getting your fit right matters more to us than getting it right the first time.

How tight is the fit around my shaft?

We build a small glue gap into every vane — about 0.525mm larger than the exact shaft diameter you enter. That's enough room for glue and a smooth slide-on, but tight enough that it seats snug and doesn't wobble before the glue sets. It's a tolerance we hold on every print, not a rough estimate.

Can I pick up locally instead of shipping?

Yes — just mention it in the notes field at checkout and we'll coordinate a pickup instead of a shipment.

The Short Version

Two Numbers Do Most of the Work

Height is what steers a broadhead back on line — taller vanes correct more, which is exactly why hunting vanes tend to stand taller than target vanes. That correction isn't free, though: taller also means more noise and, to a lesser degree, more drag.

Length works differently than most people expect. It's actually the bigger factor in aerodynamic drag — a long vane costs you more speed than a tall one does. But length also quiets things down: spread the same surface area over a longer, lower-angle curve and it slices through the air with less noise than a short, steep one. That's why a vane can be both long and quiet at the same time.

Every shape below is fully rounded already — no sharp edges anywhere in the curve, on any of them — so airflow separation and turbulence aren't a variable here. What actually separates these eight is how much length and height each one leans on, and that's what the ratings below reflect.

A Third Dial

Helical Twist

Where shape gives you a fixed amount of steering, helical twist is the dial you turn to add more — it spins the arrow in flight, which helps drive a broadhead back on line faster than a straight fletch alone. More twist means more of that correction.

It's not free, though. More twist also means more drag and more noise off the string — the same tradeoff height makes, just on a different axis. Higher settings lean harder into correction; lower settings lean toward speed and quiet.

Every order is printed to your exact twist setting, from Straight (0°) up to 30°, in either direction — so you can dial it in for a stubborn broadhead or back off for a quieter target setup, shape by shape.

The Basics

Applying and removing your vanes, in under a minute.

Step One

Applying a New Vane

Add a small ring of glue near the front of the vane's opening and another ring in the middle, then slide it down your shaft until it sits flush. Hold it in place for a few seconds while the glue sets. That's the entire process — no fletching jig, no clamps, no waiting for a full cure before moving to the next one.

Tip: a small amount of glue goes a long way — too much will just squeeze out the ends once the vane is seated.

Step Two

Removing a Damaged Vane

Nicked or bent a vane on impact? You don't need to touch the others, and you don't need to strip the whole arrow. Take a utility knife, cut down the spine that runs the length of the vane, and peel it off.

Tip: keep the blade shallow and follow the spine closely — you're cutting through the vane, not into the shaft underneath it.

The Start

Why This Exists

I'm Isaac Moser, my business has my name on it, not a made-up brand, because if a vane you ordered shows up wrong, that's on me personally, not some faceless company.

I'm based in North Dakota, where the season's short, the winters don't care what your gear was rated for, and "close enough" tends to matter a lot more than it does somewhere with a milder climate. I'm an electrical engineer by trade, which mostly just means I get genuinely annoyed when something doesn't fit right — or takes way more effort to use than it should — and I go figure out why instead of living with it.

What actually pushed me to build a business out of it was simpler than the fit problem: I was tired of ripping a vane out in the field and not having a fletching jig or the right tools on hand to fix it. So I designed these to go on fast — no jig, no clamps, just two rings of glue and a slide down the shaft. If one gets torn up mid-hunt or mid-round, cut down the spine with a utility knife, peel it off, slide a new one on, keep shooting.

The Approach

Precision Over Guesswork

Every vane you order here is built specifically for the shaft and specs you give us — not pulled off a shelf. That's the entire point of this operation. If your shaft is a slightly unusual diameter, if you run wraps, if you want a helical twist tuned for your exact broadhead setup — that's not a special request, that's just how every order works here.

"Your vane is fit to your exact arrow before it's ever printed — not the other way around."

This is a small operation, made to order, not mass-produced and warehoused. That means your order gets real attention, and it means I stand behind every single one that ships.
